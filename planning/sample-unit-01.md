## Lesson 1 — Greetings

### Learn cards

**Card 1 — New words** (tap any to hear it)

| French | English | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bonjour | hello / good morning | Literally "good day". Use it until early evening. |
| bonsoir | good evening | From about 6 pm. |
| salut | hi / bye | Casual. Works for hello *and* goodbye. |
| au revoir | goodbye | Literally "until seeing again". |
| madame | madam / Mrs | |
| monsieur | sir / Mr | Sounds like "muh-syuh". The *n* and *r* are silent. |

**Card 2 — Bonjour or salut?**
> **Bonjour** is polite and works with anyone: a shop assistant, a stranger, your boss.
> **Salut** is casual: friends, family, people your age.
> When unsure, say *bonjour*. In France, adding *madame* or *monsieur* is extra polite.
>
> - Bonjour, madame. — *Hello, madam.*
> - Salut ! — *Hi!*
> - Au revoir, monsieur. — *Goodbye, sir.*

### Practice (10 exercises, easy → hard)

1. **Pick the meaning:** *bonjour* → hello · goodbye · good evening · sir
2. **Pick the meaning:** *au revoir* → goodbye · hello · madam · hi
3. **Listen and pick:** 🔊 *bonsoir* → bonsoir · bonjour · salut
4. **Match pairs:** bonjour, bonsoir, salut, au revoir, monsieur ↔ English
5. **Listen and pick:** 🔊 *monsieur* → monsieur · madame · merci
6. **Fill the gap:** It's 9 pm. "___, madame." → Bonsoir · Bonjour
   - *Why:* after about 6 pm, say *bonsoir*.
7. **Fill the gap:** You meet your boss. "___, monsieur." → Bonjour · Salut
   - *Why:* *salut* is only for friends and family (Card 2).
8. **Build the sentence:** "Hello, madam." → [Bonjour] [madame] [salut] [monsieur]
9. **Build the sentence:** "Goodbye, sir." → [Au] [revoir] [monsieur] [bonsoir]
10. **Type it:** "goodbye" → `au revoir`

### Result
Score **8/10** · mistakes listed with the correct answer and the *why* · **Retry mistakes**

## Lesson 2 — Polite words

**New words:** merci (thank you) · s'il vous plaît (please, polite) · s'il te plaît (please, to a friend) ·
de rien (you're welcome) · pardon (sorry / excuse me) · excusez-moi (excuse me)

**Card — Two ways to say please**
> **s'il vous plaît** — polite, or to more than one person. *Un café, s'il vous plaît.*
> **s'il te plaît** — to a friend or family member.
> Both mean "if it pleases you". In texts you will see *svp* and *stp*.

**Card — Sorry and excuse me**
> **Pardon** — quick "sorry" when you bump into someone, or "pardon?" when you didn't hear.
> **Excusez-moi** — to get someone's attention before asking something.

**Example sentences:** Merci, madame. · De rien. · Excusez-moi, monsieur. · Pardon ! · Merci, salut !

## Lesson 3 — My name is…

**New words:** je m'appelle (my name is) · comment (how) · tu (you, informal) · vous (you, polite) ·
enchanté / enchantée (nice to meet you) · et (and)

**Card — Je m'appelle**
> French says "I call myself": **je m'appelle** Léa = my name is Léa.
> To ask: **Comment tu t'appelles ?** (casual) · **Comment vous appelez-vous ?** (polite)
> Learn these as whole phrases for now; the grammar behind them comes later.

**Card — Enchanté or enchantée?**
> A man says **enchanté**, a woman says **enchantée**. Both sound exactly the same.
> This extra **-e** for a woman is a pattern you will meet again and again.

**Example sentences:** Je m'appelle Paul. · Comment tu t'appelles ? · Comment vous appelez-vous ? ·
Enchantée, je m'appelle Léa. · Bonjour, je m'appelle Marc, et vous ?

## Lesson 4 — How are you?

**New words:** ça va (how's it going / it's going) · bien (well) · très (very) · pas mal (not bad) ·
et toi ? (and you? casual) · et vous ? (and you? polite)

**Card — Tu or vous?**
> French has two words for "you".
> **tu** — one friend, family member, child or pet.
> **vous** — a stranger, someone older, at work; also *always* for more than one person.
> When unsure, use **vous**. If they say *tu* to you, you can switch.

**Card — Ça va ?**
> **Ça va ?** is a question when your voice goes up, and an answer when it goes down.
> — Ça va ? — Ça va bien, merci. Et toi ?
> — Très bien ! — Pas mal.

**Example sentences:** Salut, ça va ? · Ça va bien, merci. · Très bien, et vous ? · Pas mal, et toi ? ·
Bonjour, madame, ça va ?
